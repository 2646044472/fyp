## Top two formalizations

### B1: compound-domain attribution certificate (P2 boundary)

- **[D]** A frozen matcher produces pair scores `s`; `d` denotes broad source/target domain, `v` the claimed device/sensor factor, `u` the recorded nuisance vector (pose, illumination, distance, ROI/cropping path, surface condition), and `y` the genuine/impostor label. The report action is `attribute v`, `attribute only d`, or `insufficient support`.
- **[A]** Factor-specific attribution requires matched or cross-classified support over `u`, a predeclared target FAR/FRR rule, and an identity-disjoint split. A pre-cropped ROI cannot be silently treated as the raw target capture path.
- **[T]** A factor-specific conclusion is admissible only if its conditional estimate and a predeclared sensitivity/bound analysis exclude an effect reversal. Otherwise the correct observable outcome is an attribution-insufficient certificate, even if broad-domain EER differs.
- **Observable outcome:** support table for `(v,u)`, cross-domain EER/FAR/FRR, conditional effect interval, sensitivity-bound interval, and Pi runtime only for the frozen matcher.
- **Counterexample:** two acquisition-generating processes with identical scanner-versus-phone score distributions but opposite device effects because lighting/pose/ROI co-vary differently. The observed broad-domain result cannot select between them.
- **Negative-result value:** it defeats the claim that P2's public ROI experiment demonstrates a sensor/device or template-protection barrier, while retaining a reproducible descriptive domain-shift finding.

### B2: robustness--linkability conclusion stability (P1 boundary)

- **[D]** Each identity has device-indexed protected/unprotected templates `t(i,d,k)` under a frozen extractor and predeclared transform/key condition `k`. The decision vector is `(EER_d, linkability_a)`, where `a` is a declared two-template or multiple-score observation model.
- **[A]** Identity and device are blocked in development/test; the exact transform, key policy, thresholds, and attacker score observations are frozen before target scoring. EER and linkability are separate endpoints, not proxies for one another.
- **[T]** A deployment conclusion is transportable only when the sign/order of its comparison to baseline is stable across device-blocked resamples and the two predeclared `a` models. A reversal is the result; it does not justify selecting a new method.
- **Observable outcome:** EER/FNMR at frozen FAR, two linkability estimates with intervals, conclusion-stability rate, template size, and optional Pi latency/RAM.
- **Counterexample:** the same transform improves device-held-out EER but changes from low to high measured linkability when the observation model changes from one pair to multiple scores; a single reported score cannot settle the privacy decision.
- **Negative-result value:** establishes that P1 cannot be sold as an uncomplicated robustness/privacy trade-off on the available protocol; it yields a disciplined evaluation axis for any later authorised P2 comparison.

## Decision

**PIVOT.** Do not replace the already challenged P2-CTB with another generic biometric method. B1 is the only defensible next *research gate*: an authorised, offline attribution-sufficiency audit can produce a useful negative result without generating or presenting biometric attack material. B2 is a secondary protocol audit only if paired data, a published transform, and the multi-template threat model are all licensed and frozen. B3--B6 should remain controls, conformance notes, or explicit kills. Before any active-direction change, validation should try to kill B1 by finding a direct causal/benchmark-attribution neighbor and by checking whether X-Palm metadata actually permits the required conditional support analysis.
