# Divergence Packet: 2026-08-29 Sensing-action identifiability

## Decision investigated

Find a low-cost, non-personal, non-safety-actuating edge-sensing FYP whose unit of contribution is a falsifiable **measurement-action decision**, rather than a new fusion model, a Raspberry Pi deployment, a modality swap, or an adaptive-sampling rule.  This packet treats every possible innovation claim as **Amber**.  It does not claim that a gap in these searches establishes novelty.

The proposed bounded story is deliberately low stakes.  A laboratory technician uses a local RGB plus small ToF station to record whether a standard, non-personal component is in the required pose/geometry class.  The station may accept the record, request one declared re-acquisition through a manual two-position fixture, request a capture of a fixed reference patch, or send the episode to manual review.  A false accepted record causes an audit/re-run of a bench experiment or maintenance log; an unnecessary action consumes a stated operator-time and capture budget.  No result authorizes motion, safety control, or a production decision.

## Search boundary

- Search date: 2026-08-29.  Queries appear below.
- Project constraints used: one year; runnable benchtop demo by 2026-12; affordable self-purchased parts permitted; later 2027-H1 repetition/optimization; no personal data, production service, hazardous experiment, or safety actuation.
- Mandatory direct-neighbor checks: adaptive edge sensing/allocation; physical ToF limitations; robust missing-modality control; online RGB/ToF calibration; active sensing/fault recovery.
- Primary/official sources were preferred.  Where a full paper was unavailable, the gap is labelled `[GAP]` rather than treated as absence evidence.

## Top two formalizations

### `SAP`: state-separated action protocol

- **[D] Decision.**  For episode `t`, select `a_t` from `{accept, reference-patch check, second-fixture-pose capture, manual review}` before committing the local record.
- **[A] Action and loss.**  `reference-patch check` is evidence about the currently mounted sensing chain only; `second-fixture-pose` is evidence about target observability only; manual review is the conservative terminal action.  The predeclared loss is `false_accept_cost * I(false accept) + action_cost(a_t) + latency_cost`, measured with one action budget per episode.
- **[T] Observable outcome.**  The policy observes RGB/ToF measurements, declared device/timing telemetry and inexpensive capture metadata.  Independent fixtures/reference measurements supply labels for (i) mounting/device/timing condition, (ii) scene/target observability condition, and (iii) final task correctness.  Labels are not available to the online policy.
- **[C] Falsifiable claim.**  On predeclared held-out *crossed* cells (device/mount condition x target material/pose/background), the state-separated policy has lower false-accept/action-cost loss than the three fixed baselines.  This is not a claim of universal diagnosis, cross-device transfer, or a new fusion backbone.
- **Counterexample.**  If the raw and telemetry observations carry no action-relevant information beyond a quality score, the best quality-only router plus fixed retry can match the policy.  If a reference patch changes none of the uncertainty relevant to the target, it cannot license `accept`.
- **Negative-result value.**  A result that the quality-only/fixed policy is equal or better is useful: it bounds when explicit cause/observability separation does *not* buy a better action under this specified sensor and task family.

### `AIV`: action-identifiability-first sensing

- **[D] Decision.**  Choose `generic repeat`, a specific diagnostic capture, or `manual review` when the same symptom may arise from sensor/timing state or a hard-but-healthy scene.
- **[A] Action and loss.**  Define `u(z,a)` for the finite physical/scene state `z` and action `a`.  States are grouped only when they share the loss-minimising action, not when they have the same physical root cause.  A new capture is permitted only when a fixed before/after observation discriminates two groups with different best actions.
- **[T] Observable outcome.**  `x` is the current low-cost sensor/telemetry tuple; `x'` is one declared additional capture.  The analysis reports an empirical conditional confusion matrix and action regret on held-out physical cells, rather than claiming causal identification from correlation.
- **[C] Falsifiable claim.**  An action-equivalence policy uses fewer unproductive recaptures at the same false-accept rate than generic repeat, and improves loss over a root-cause classifier that is forced to act from the same observables and budget.
- **Counterexample.**  Two conditions yielding the same distribution of `(x,x')` but different optimal actions are not distinguishable by any such policy.  The correct output is `manual review`; a causal name for the fault would be unsupported.
- **Negative-result value.**  The resulting finite action-identifiability map says which pairs of realistic partial degradations cannot be solved by the available low-cost observations.  It is not a global impossibility theorem.

## Decision

- `SAP` - **HOLD.**  It is the only candidate here that changes the evaluation unit from robustness of a model to an auditable, bounded sensing-action protocol.  Its three novelty audits remain Amber: component collision is known (MAMMOTH, active diagnosis); exact-claim collision is unresolved; the observation/action-identifiability boundary is explicit rather than hidden.
- `AIV` - **PIVOT / HOLD.**  It is a sharper possible formalisation of `SAP`, not an independent claim to stack on top of it.  It is valuable only if validation can show that action-equivalence, rather than generic root-cause diagnosis or generic retry, changes the endpoint.
- `CVB`, `AFE`, `RCP` - **KILL.**  Their central mechanism is already directly represented by inspected calibration, task-aware front-end, or adaptive smart-sensing work.

**Overall: PIVOT.**  No direction is promoted.  Send `SAP`/`AIV` to an adversarial direct-neighbor and identifiability audit before changing the active register.  The decisive next query is whether recent primary work already combines partial physical degradation, distinct sensor/scene/task labels, explicit recovery/reacquisition actions and an action-cost/risk endpoint on a bounded sensing task.
