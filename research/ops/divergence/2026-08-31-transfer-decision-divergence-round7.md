# Divergence Packet: 2026-08-31 transfer decision under new device, load, and fault cells

## Decision investigated

The user-recorded question is: **"a low-cost edge sensing/reliability method was tuned on some equipment; after it moves to a new device, a new load, or a new fault condition, does it still retain decision value?"**

This packet looks for a thesis-sized answer that changes a real decision, rather than a new classifier, a Raspberry Pi deployment, test-time adaptation (TTA), an active sensing mechanism, a camera chart, a generic benchmark, or a storage-recovery test.  The permitted setting is a non-personal, non-production, non-safety benchtop system; inexpensive parts may be purchased; the demo date is 2026-12 and the remaining 2027-H1 period can support repetitions and held-out cells.  Every proposed distinction is Amber until component collision, exact-claim collision, and boundary/impossibility audits have all passed.

## Top two formalizations

### TRC-X: a release decision with an explicit sampling population

- **[D] Decision:** for a frozen edge record/alert policy, issue `release`, `trial-only`, or `do-not-release` before it is copied to a new member of a predeclared hardware family.
- **[A] Allowed action:** run a finite, pre-registered selection of labelled acceptance trials.  A labelled trial has a named unit, a named benign load/operating state, a named reversible injected fault state, and an independent test oracle.  The policy is frozen; no TTA, active sensing, or source/target retuning is allowed.
- **[T] Target:** the policy's decision loss on an explicitly specified finite or sampled population \(\mathcal P=\mathcal D\times\mathcal L\times\mathcal F\), not on arbitrary future devices.  The target could be a one-sided upper confidence bound on false retained records, or a predeclared maximum expected loss.
- **Observable outcome:** trial-level \((d,l,f,z,\ell)\), where \(z\) is the node's frozen output and \(\ell\) is independent oracle loss.  It does *not* observe unrestricted future fault mechanisms, a population distribution outside \(\mathcal P\), or unlabelled target truth.
- **Counterexample:** with one tested device but many load/fault runs, a unit-specific effect can make the pooled lower bound appear favourable while a new unit fails every cell.  The trial data do not identify a device-family claim unless the device sampling rule and number of distinct units are part of the estimand.  Conversely, if a standard crossed/stratified reliability demonstration plan gives the same release rule, TRC-X offers no contribution.
- **Negative-result value:** an honest result is that the affordable number of units cannot support a device-transfer release claim, while still quantifying source-unit and fixed-cell performance.  This directly answers the user's saved question without pretending a model survived unseen hardware.

### UOD-ReleaseBound: why no-witness transfer claims should stop at `unknown`

- **[D] Decision:** `continue`, `manual-check`, or `suspend` a non-personal local record stream after an unlabelled device/load/fault shift.
- **[A] Allowed action:** choose the three-way output based on a finite window of local inputs, frozen predictions and normal runtime telemetry.  No labels, reference measurement, delayed review, active probe, second sensor, model update or retraining enter the boundary.
- **[T] Target:** a non-vacuous distribution-free upper bound on the fixed model's true target decision loss over the next window.
- **Observable outcome:** \(O=(x_{1:n},h(x_{1:n}),r_{1:n})\).  The candidate must not silently assume that telemetry identifies \(p(y\mid x)\).
- **Counterexample:** choose two target worlds with the same distribution of \(O\), and reverse labels on a positive-probability input subset.  The source and target risk order changes but every permitted observation is unchanged; a policy must therefore act identically in both worlds.  This is an instance of the general unlabeled-accuracy identification boundary, not a universal statement about all diagnostic systems.
- **Negative-result value:** it says precisely what evidence is missing and prevents an unjustified `continue` guarantee.  It becomes an actual FYP only if a later audit identifies a meaningful minimal witness or restricted-shift condition that is not already a known result.

Failed/negative findings:

- No inspected source supports a distribution-free target-risk guarantee from ordinary local inputs, model outputs and telemetry after unrestricted device/load/fault transfer.  The existing impossibility result is a stronger reason to refuse a positive claim than a lack of search hits.
- No clear primary 2024-2026 exact neighbor for a *cluster-aware, finite crossed-cell transfer-release* protocol was verified in this pass.  This is an unresolved direct-neighbor gap, not a novelty claim; reliability demonstration and cross-machine diagnosis are both strong component-collision risks.
- The cheap physical implementations considered here either require multiple nominally identical units and an independent fault/record oracle, or collapse into simulation.  A one-unit bench cannot test a new-device statement.

## Decision

**PIVOT.**  No candidate is promoted.  **MFD-Release** and **MQ-Guard** are KILL as direct fault-diagnosis/calibration continuations.  **UOD-ReleaseBound** and **FIP-Transfer** are PIVOT only as useful negative-result studies, not positive mechanisms.  **TRC-X** is the sole HOLD lead: it could become a defensible *bounded release-decision / evidence-sufficiency* FYP only if a validation round finds a real distinction from published reliability demonstration testing and confirms that 4-6 independently sampled low-cost units plus an independent oracle can support the stated estimand.  Its innovation remains Amber, not claimed.
