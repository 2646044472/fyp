## Top two formalizations

### 1. `TRIG-LEAK`: deadline-constrained event privacy

- `[D]` A deadline `D`, byte/energy budget `B`, event arrival process, and a passive observer who sees packet timing/length but not payload.
- `[A]` `send-now`, fixed-clock cover, event-DP shaping, or causal defer/cover policy.
- `[T]` The event-time leakage/cost frontier: observer advantage or AUC, late-notice probability, bytes, and energy.
- Observable outcome: an attacker trained on defended traces and an independently logged notice-deadline outcome.
- Counterexample: the fixed periodic schedule achieves equal or better leakage at equal late-notice and byte cost; or two event processes are indistinguishable under the declared budget.
- Negative-result value: closes the proposed adaptive schedule without pretending that a board-level implementation is a privacy contribution.

### 2. `PART-ACT`: action-risk-aware local/split execution

- `[D]` A fixed capture payload, local and remote compute options, measured link/CPU trace, energy budget, and an action loss assigning higher cost to an incorrect commit than to a retry/defer.
- `[A]` Local-only, remote-only, fixed partition, EDF, latency-only selector, or action-risk-aware selector.
- `[T]` Pareto frontier of wrong-action probability, p95 completion time, and joules at matched task quality and workload.
- Observable outcome: exact-payload oracle labels the downstream action independently of the selector.
- Counterexample: latency-only or fixed partition dominates; the remote service has no independent benefit; or network delay and model error cannot be separated.
- Negative-result value: a reproducible finite benchmark showing that action-aware selection adds no value under the tested edge regime.
