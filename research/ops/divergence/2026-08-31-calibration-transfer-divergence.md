# Divergence Packet: 2026-08-31 Cross-device / Cross-illumination Transfer

## Decision investigated

Generate new low-cost Raspberry Pi 5 RGB + NoIR directions around transfer and adaptive sensing. The unit of contribution is a falsifiable *retain / reacquire / calibrate* decision, not a new network or a Raspberry Pi deployment. All proposals are Amber until component collision, exact-claim collision, and boundary/impossibility audits are complete.

## Recent-paper limitation map

* [K] Baek et al., *Adaptive Camera Sensor for Vision Models*, arXiv:2503.02170v3 (19 May 2026), Secs. 1, 2.1-2.2, 3.1-3.2, 5. Lens selects camera parameters by a model-specific confidence proxy, with candidate-selection algorithms and fixed/random/auto-exposure baselines (Secs. 3.1-3.2, 5). The paper reports that confidence can be a useful proxy but its benchmark uses one physical camera, manually varied ISO/shutter/aperture and six light options (Secs. 4-5). This occupies generic preview-to-exposure/model-confidence control; an external witness or a code-level action endpoint would have to be materially different.
* [K] Yue and Wei, *Effective cross-sensor color constancy using a dual-mapping strategy*, JOSA A 41, 329-337 (published 26 Jan 2024), abstract and Tables 1-3. The method needs white points from training/testing sensors under D65 and predicts illuminants with a 0.003 MB MLP; it reports cross-sensor angular-error results and no target-sensor scene collection (https://opg.optica.org/josaa/abstract.cfm?uri=josaa-41-2-329, lines 268-271, 359-412). Thus “map RGB to NoIR colour” is already occupied, while downstream reliability/action and non-D65 physical labels remain unverified.
* [K] Punnappurath et al., *Improved Mapping Between Illuminations and Sensors for RAW Images*, arXiv:2508.14730v1 (20 Aug 2025), Secs. 1-3 and Appendix C. The dataset has 390 illuminations, four cameras and 18 scenes; an MLP maps illumination-to-illumination or sensor-to-sensor RAW responses (Sec. 1, lines 3-5, 15-29). The authors explicitly caution that diagonal color transforms are not realistic for non-neutral materials and that a single X-Rite chart under-represents real materials (Sec. 2, lines 34-40; Sec. 3). A six-patch or material panel used for an exact-code decision would still need to beat this direct mapping family.
* [K] Zhang et al., *Cost-efficient Active Illumination Camera for Hyper-spectral Reconstruction*, arXiv:2406.19560v1 (27 Jun 2024), Secs. 3, 5 and 6.4.1. It uses a low-cost active multi-band camera and discusses calibration/misalignment constraints. This is a direct neighbor for IR/illumination actuation, so any claimed adaptive illumination must use a distinct decision endpoint and independent labels.
* [K] Baek et al. (Lens), Sec. 2.2, lines 105-109, states prior sensor-control work is limited by constrained environments and that more realistic benchmarks are needed. This is evidence for a bounded physical benchmark, not proof of a gap; later work or an equivalent simple baseline can kill every candidate below.

## Top two formalizations

### XFER-ACT

* **[D] Data-generating process:** a fixed printed payload is captured by RGB or NoIR, under a declared lamp/material/fault cell; source-device calibration episodes and target-device episodes are disjoint. The exact payload decoded from a flatbed/phone oracle is independent of the Pi decision.
* **[A] Assumptions:** static target; manual or logged exposure/white balance; finite action set (a\in\{RGB, NoIR+IR, two-shot\}); matched capture-energy budget; no claim beyond the purchased pair and sampled lamps.
* **[T] Testable claim:** Spearman/Kendall ordering of action false-retain risk on held-out NoIR and lamp cells is preserved by a source-calibrated policy better than fixed two-shot and scalar-quality controls. A preregistered rank inversion is the positive mechanism-level result; no inversion is a valid negative result.
* **Observable outcome:** exact-code accuracy, false-retain, `unknown`/reacquisition, latency, joules, and per-cell action rank.
* **Counterexample:** two worlds produce identical RGB preview, lux and metadata but one has transparent-cover glare and the other low visible light; opposite optimal actions make transfer unidentifiable. This must be reported, not averaged away.
* **Negative-result value:** a finite audit showing that cross-device calibration or synthetic robustness does not transport action rankings, or that it does, is reusable evidence for future edge deployments.

### LUX-VOI

* **[D] Data-generating process:** equal-lux lamps with different spectra, plus low/high lux levels, independently measured by a calibrated reference meter; each episode has a physical cover/clean label and exact marker oracle.
* **[A] Assumptions:** lux sensor is temporally aligned and not saturated; IR illuminator is eye-safe and logged; no moving targets; image-only baselines receive the same preview and timing budget.
* **[T] Testable claim:** at a fixed 95th-percentile false-retain bound, adding lux to a frozen image-only policy either reduces expected joules/latency or increases exact-code retention on held-out spectral cells. If not, the lux channel is instrumentation only.
* **Observable outcome:** matched-cost risk-coverage and energy frontier, plus lux-versus-spectrum residuals.
* **Counterexample:** equal lux but different spectral power distributions produce opposite RGB/NoIR utility; a scalar lux witness cannot identify the best action.
* **Negative-result value:** an explicit result that lux is insufficient prevents unjustified ambient-sensor purchases and documents the boundary between photometric level and spectral state.
