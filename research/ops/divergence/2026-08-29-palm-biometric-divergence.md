# Divergence Packet: 2026-08-29 palm/biometric edge directions

## Decision investigated

The user listed six concepts while saying “four”; I treated all six separately: (1) cross-device robustness versus privacy/linkability, (2) transferability of template-reconstruction attacks, (3) protected templates on heterogeneous edge devices, (4) adaptive black-box attacks using matcher feedback, (5) quality/uncertainty-aware authentication under unseen devices and degradation, and (6) cross-spectral or missing-modality robustness. The question is which of these can become a one-year, low-cost, non-production palm/biometric edge FYP with a claim that a direct baseline can falsify. This packet deliberately does not defend the locked I2C direction.

## Recent-paper limitation map

* **Cross-device adaptation is already an explicit palm task.** AdaptPFL (IJCAI-25, pp. 7074-7082) models phone-brand and lighting drift with feature decoupling and adaptive aggregation; its experiments use XJTU-UP, MPD and a multispectral set (100–500 volunteers), but do not measure template linkability, revocation, attack transfer, or edge energy/latency. Its identity/context independence assumption is a [GAP] rather than a guarantee.
* **Privacy-preserving cross-spectral FL is also direct prior art.** DPFed-Palm (IEEE TIP 34, 2025, pp. 4885-4895, DOI 10.1109/TIP.2025.3592508) combines local losses, FedAvg/PFL and dynamic cross-spectral weights on PolyU, IITD and CASIA. The abstract reports recognition/privacy gains, not an adversarial template attack, ISO unlinkability test, or measured low-cost-device budget. Replacing its aggregator with another one would be a model-swap thesis.
* **Cancelable palmprint papers report the requested EER/security tuple already.** Ali et al. (EURASIP Journal on Information Security, version of record 5 Oct 2024) report (D_{\leftrightarrow}^{sys}=0.01), 100% identification/0% EER in a legitimate setting and 99.752%/0.31% in a same-token worst case. Sections 3-4 use controlled Tongji/PolyU data and random projections; Section 5 lists multimodal fusion and deeper models as future work. This is a strong baseline, but not heterogeneous hardware or a stress protocol.
* **Linkability under multiple scores has a formal direct neighbor.** Otroshi Shahreza, Shkel and Marcel, IEEE Access 12 (2024), DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3433536, define maximal linkability when an attacker has several similarity scores and evaluate five composition scenarios. The result is modality/protection agnostic and does not establish palm edge behavior; it makes a simplistic two-template unlinkability claim unsafe.
* **Distance preservation itself can defeat protected-template claims.** “On the security risk of pre-image attack on cancelable biometrics” (Journal of King Saud University Computer and Information Sciences 36(5), 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.jksuci.2024.102060) gives single- and cross-transformation attacks under Kerckhoffs assumptions and demonstrates leakage on face, iris and fingerprint schemes. It does not test palmprint or a physical edge implementation, leaving a narrow replication/transfer question but killing generic “our hash is irreversible” wording.
* **Template-reconstruction transfer is demonstrated for faces.** Shahreza, George and Marcel, arXiv:2411.03960 (6 Nov 2024), Sections 3.1, 4.2, 5 and 6, assume a leaked embedding, black-box access to the victim extractor, and ability to inject a reconstructed image into a target system. Their adapter to Arc2Face (trained on 42M faces) transfers reconstructions across models, but transfer success is lower than same-system success and the compute/data threat model is heavy for an undergraduate palm study.
* **Query-feedback black-box attacks are established outside palm.** DifAttack (AAAI-24, pp. 3666-3674, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v38i4.28156) optimizes a disentangled adversarial feature using victim query feedback and claims open-set transfer. FeatureBA (Expert Systems with Applications 276, 2025, 127082) reports up to 99.73% fewer queries using surrogate intermediate features and includes face-recognition experiments. Both are digital attacks; neither supplies a palm sensor protocol, physical perturbation safety, or edge query-cost model.
* **Uncertainty and open-set rejection are mature for faces.** UCFace (Ahn et al., IEEE TIFS 19, 2024, pp. 7176-7186, DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2024.3426973) uses probabilistic embeddings and image-quality-derived uncertainty for low-resolution open-set faces. Gallery-Aware Uncertainty Estimation (Erlygin & Zaytsev, arXiv:2408.14229, 26 Aug 2024) shows embedding variance alone misses gallery overlap. PalmRSS (Jia et al., Pattern Recognition 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.patcog.2025.111620) already targets single-source domain generalization for palm biometrics with Fourier alignment/histogram matching. Thus “add a quality score and reject” is not a gap; an edge action/cost endpoint is needed.
* **Missing modality and cross-spectral palm are now direct tasks.** SSFD-Net (Pan et al., Digital Signal Processing 159, 2025, 105003, DOI 10.1016/j.dsp.2025.105003) reconstructs palmvein features when palmprint or palmvein is missing and evaluates three multimodal benchmarks (Sections 3-5). DPFed-Palm handles cross-spectral non-IID clients. X-Palm (Seyedmohammadi et al., arXiv:2606.08437, 7 Jun 2026) releases 6,006 paired multispectral/smartphone images from 103 people and reports severe collapse of 12 SOTA models on compound shifts. A new fusion network or dataset-only benchmark would collide directly; a predeclared sensor-failure action with cost and abstention may still be testable.

## Top two formalizations

### P2: leaked-template transfer across sensors

* **[D]** Enrollment embedding (e_A=f_A(x_A)) from sensor/model A is leaked; attacker has black-box query access to (f_A), a public surrogate palm set, and a target sensor/model B. No live deployment or personal data is used.
* **[A]** Train an inverter (g(e_A)), optionally adapt it to B with a small public calibration set, then submit reconstructed image (\hat{x}) to frozen B. Action is a bounded digital/camera-recapture probe, not bypassing a service.
* **[T]** Targeted impersonation after template leakage; compare same-model, cross-model, and cross-sensor transfer under a fixed query/compute budget.
* **Observable outcome:** attack acceptance rate at the pre-frozen FAR threshold, reconstruction identity similarity, sensor-specific degradation, and confidence intervals.
* **Counterexample:** a reconstruction that matches A but falls to impostor FAR on B, or a simple pixel/feature nearest-neighbor baseline that performs equally well.
* **Negative-result value:** establishes a finite transfer barrier and identifies which sensor normalization or protected-template choice blocks transfer.

### P5: uncertainty-gated edge authentication

* **[D]** Each episode contains a claimed identity, gallery, RGB palm frame, device ID, measured brightness/blur, and optional NIR frame; test identities and devices are disjoint from training. Ground truth is the captured identity, including unknown identities.
* **[A]** The edge node emits `accept`, `reacquire` (one additional frame or NIR wake), or `manual/unknown` under a fixed review and energy budget. Thresholds are frozen on a development split.
* **[T]** Compound domain shift (unseen camera + low light/blur + open-set user) with a risk-sensitive objective (L = c_{FA}FA + c_{FR}FR + c_R\,review + c_E\,energy).
* **Observable outcome:** risk-coverage and expected cost at matched review/energy budgets, calibration error, and cross-device/open-set confidence intervals.
* **Counterexample:** score-only thresholding or a single quality metric reaches the same risk-coverage; then uncertainty adds no information.
* **Negative-result value:** a calibrated map of when abstention/reacquisition is not worth its edge cost, avoiding an unsupported “uncertainty improves security” claim.

## Decision

P2 and P5 are the only candidates that currently have a clean falsifiable edge endpoint without inventing a new model: transfer success under leaked-template cross-sensor attacks, and risk/cost under uncertainty-gated accept/reacquire/abstain. P1, P3 and P6 are HOLD pending hardware/data and direct-neighbor audits; P4 is HOLD pending an explicitly sandboxed, non-abusive threat model. No candidate receives a global novelty claim.
