# Divergence Packet: 2026-10-05 HKSL and edge direction search

## Decision investigated

**Decision:** whether to reopen the broader edge-computing FYP search with Hong Kong Sign Language (HKSL) recognition as a serious candidate, despite the active charter's Palm-only scope lock and the later charter note reopening a broader edge search. The scope conflict is for the coordinator/user to resolve; this packet does not change the scope decision or active files.

HKSL is a signed language, not spoken Cantonese rendered as hand gestures. The research task would be HKSL continuous sign-language recognition (CSLR) or translation. The TVB-HKSL-News authors state that spoken subtitles are not valid monotonic gloss targets because HKSL sentence structure and grammar differ; fluent HKSL signers annotated the glosses (Niu et al. 2024, Sec. 4, pp. 640–641).

**Initial decision:** HOLD. There is a real local-language story and a public research benchmark, but not yet an FYP-ready positive contribution: the main corpus has only two signers, the original edge idea is already directly occupied at the level of deployment demos, streaming and privacy each have direct neighbors, and data use is not established. Do not call the direction novel from this search.

## Recent-paper limitation map

- **HKSL corpus / label fit [K]:** TVB-HKSL-News (LREC-COLING 2024) contributes 16.07 hours, two signers, 6,515 glosses, and a 34.08% test WER baseline. Its stated experimental emphasis includes signer-dependent training; the authors' best results use substantial data from a single signer. The models were trained on eight V100 GPUs. The paper does not establish a Pi-ready model or broad signer generalization. The paper says subtitles cannot directly substitute for sign glosses; human HKSL signers provided verbatim gloss labels. (Niu et al., Secs. 1, 3–5, pp. 636–644.)
- **Data permission [K/GAP]:** the separate HKSL Corpus says video/transcription access requires registration and consent; some materials are restricted. The TVB-HKSL licence request form currently published by HKUST contains an end date of 2025-09-14. As of this search date, neither a current extension nor permission for a new FYP is verified. This is a go/no-go gate, not an assumption that the paper's historical release remains usable.
- **Edge deployment [K]:** IEEE's 2024 SAS record reports isolated ASL gesture classification on an STM32F746/Cortex-M7, comparing MobileNet/TFLM/STM32Cube.AI. Its task and language differ from continuous HKSL. IEEE's 2026 EVST abstract reports a Raspberry Pi Zero 2W real-time translator at 92% accuracy/270 ms, but its abstract describes a gesture/translation system and does not establish HKSL CSLR, signer-independent generalization, or independently audited language quality. It directly defeats “edge sign demo” as a contribution by itself.
- **Online processing [K]:** Zuo, Wei & Mak (arXiv:2401.05336v1, 2024) already propose online CSLR through a learned sign dictionary, sliding-window isolated-sign recognition, duplicate suppression, and online SLT extension, tested on three benchmarks. Kermani et al. (ALVR 2026) directly study online sign-text streaming policy, with pause detection, linguistic readiness, and a synthetic ASL discourse set. A new HKSL FYP cannot claim online recognition or temporal pause gating as novel. Both papers leave a language/domain transfer and real-world interaction question, but “another language” alone is dataset transfer, not a CS contribution.
- **Privacy and non-manual cues [K]:** sign video contains identity-bearing face/body information, while facial movement conveys language. Bragg et al. (ASSETS 2020) studied signer participation and privacy distortions; DiffSLVA (2023) performs sign-video anonymization; Pham & Avramidis (IVA Adjunct 2025) find mouth information useful; and Xue et al. (IEEE TMM 2026, DOI record/abstract inspected) propose PrivSign for privacy-preserving CSLR. Generic pose-only conversion, face blur, or RGB anonymization is therefore occupied and can erase language cues. A genuinely distinct user-controlled retention/release task would need separate threat, attacker, utility endpoint, and consent evidence.
- **Signer robustness/adaptation [K]:** signer removal/consistency constraints (Zuo & Mak, 2022/2024) and cross-lingual auxiliary training (Wei et al., ICCV 2023) predate this search. TVB-HKSL-News has only two signers, so a robust unseen-signer claim cannot be estimated from that benchmark alone. HKSL Corpus access could expand examples, but participant counts, label policy, licence, and signer-held-out splits remain unknown.
- **Advisor fit [K]:** Bob Zhang's checked public work includes principal-line alignment and heterogeneous/multiview palmprint recognition. I found no direct Bob Zhang HKSL/SLR neighbor in this bounded search. That means low demonstrated advisor-topic adjacency, not evidence of novelty. Closest sign-language authors from the checked benchmark are Brian Mak/Ronglai Zuo; advisor availability/support remains [GAP].

## Top two formalizations

### 1. HKSL-STREAM: incremental decision quality under a fixed edge budget

- **[D] Decision:** for each incoming local video prefix, either emit a provisional gloss/text update, wait for more frames, or abstain/ask the hearing partner to confirm. A finalization is an action that can alter the service interaction; it is not just a sequence score.
- **[A] Assumptions:** continuous HKSL video, timestamps, signer-held-out split, consent to process locally, expert gloss/reference annotation, fixed hardware/power budget, and a predeclared latency deadline. Do not infer HKSL translation quality from Cantonese subtitles.
- **[T] Claim to test:** at a fixed false-finalization ceiling, one named local streaming policy improves the observed latency-versus-WER/unknown frontier over fixed Wait-k, fixed pause threshold, and final offline decode on the released/consented test cohort.
- **Observable outcome:** per-prefix gloss edits, false finalization, WER, time-to-useful-output, abstention/confirmation rate, Pi latency/memory/energy, all per signer and episode.
- **Counterexample:** pauses correlate with clauses in the existing set but fail on a signer whose pacing differs; an LLM readiness head uses future/context leakage; a fixed delay achieves the same quality at lower cost; available HKSL clips are already segmented and cannot support real streaming.
- **Negative-result value:** establishes that current HKSL data/labels do not identify a useful real-time edge action or that simple fixed policies dominate. This is more useful than reporting only accuracy/FPS.

### 2. HKSL-PRIV-RELEASE: finite privacy–language–resource frontier

- **[D] Decision:** after local inference, choose which artifact can be retained or shared: raw video, transformed video/keypoints, or nothing. The default assumption is no cloud sharing; any broader threat must be separately specified.
- **[A] Assumptions:** an explicit attacker with access to the retained artifact, a defined identity-matching gallery and metric, independent signers, expert language labels, and informed Deaf participant consent/acceptability review. Facial non-manual features are language signal, not expendable identity decoration.
- **[T] Claim to test:** within a declared HKSL sample and resource budget, at least one representation has a strictly better measured identity-linkage versus recognition/action-cost frontier than baselines, with the bound repeated on unseen signers. No population privacy guarantee follows from this finite sample.
- **Observable outcome:** attacker top-k/linkage performance, gloss WER/action errors, latency, energy, stored bytes, participant acceptability, with per-signer uncertainty.
- **Counterexample:** face blur destroys mouth information; pose-only misses hand shape or non-manual grammar; stylized video remains linkable by clothing/background; an always-delete rule gives better privacy and adequate utility; direct PrivSign/other anonymization closes the frontier.
- **Negative-result value:** a bounded demonstration that current local representations cannot jointly support the declared language utility and threat model, useful for consent/retention design but not a new privacy algorithm.

- `Cantonese sign language recognition Hong Kong dataset continuous sign language recognition paper` found TVB-HKSL-News and the HKSL Corpus; “Cantonese sign language” appears to conflate spoken Cantonese with HKSL. Reframed search as `Hong Kong Sign Language (HKSL)`.

- `sign language recognition edge device real time paper Raspberry Pi CSLR` found MCU ASL and Pi Zero demos, but no evidence in this bounded search of a full, signer-independent HKSL CSLR pipeline benchmarked on Pi.
- `primary paper online streaming continuous sign language recognition latency streaming signer adaptation` found Zuo et al. 2024 and Kermani et al. 2026; therefore generic “streaming on edge” and pause-aware emission are not open claims based on this search.
- `sign language recognition privacy preserving facial blur signer video privacy primary paper` found Bragg et al., DiffSLVA, facial-cue evaluation, and a 2026 PrivSign record. It is not defensible to pitch face blurring, keypoint-only input, or generic anonymization as new.
- `Bob Zhang Hong Kong Polytechnic University research sign language` and adjacent PolyU publication searches found palmprint/multiview papers but no direct sign-language connection. Search coverage is incomplete.
- Failed/limited access: IEEE full text for the 2024/2026 edge articles was not available; official record/abstract only. The 2026 PrivSign publisher full paper was not retrieved, so its exact assumptions and limitations need validation. The HKSL Corpus clips and a current TVB licence were not obtained.

## Decision

**HOLD.** Keep HKSL edge recognition as an exploratory direction only if the coordinator/user explicitly reopens the current Palm-only scope and a current lawful data/annotation route is established. Among the four candidates, HKSL-STREAM has the clearest decision story, but it is only a candidate for a bounded interaction/latency evaluation after full-data, label, compute, and Deaf-community feasibility checks; current online CSLR/streaming work defeats a broad novelty claim. HKSL-PRIV-RELEASE and HKSL-LOWSHOT have attractive harms but higher collision and data/ethics risks. HKSL-EDGE-BASELINE is a useful reproduction appendix, not a thesis mechanism.

The next research gate is not model implementation: confirm scope with the user/coordinator, identify a live licence and sufficient signer-disjoint HKSL data, and obtain language-community/advisor support. If those fail, kill the HKSL branch before spending the FYP window. No active charter/register/archive file was changed.

HOLD
