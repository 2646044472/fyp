# Divergence Packet: 2026-10-05 Palm Alternative Ideas

## Decision investigated

**HOLD.** The user asked for alternatives to the current conditional `E-BOUND-X` direction. This packet examines four other Palm-only ideas. They span physical sensing attacks, cross-hand identity, template privacy, and missing-modality reliability. None is ready to replace the current route: each has a close direct neighbor, and the strongest distinctions currently concern the local capture/action endpoint rather than a new recognition method.

## Recent-paper limitation map

- **Open-set/domain shifts:** PalmBridge [K] studies open-set palmprint feature alignment and cross-dataset generalization. Its protocols are dataset gallery/probe evaluations; it does not measure the local Pi capture-to-mock-action path. This occupies generic "domain shift hurts open-set palm recognition; align embeddings" claims. Bob Zhang is a coauthor, so it is also a direct advisor-neighbor check.
- **Physical recognition attacks:** CAAP [K] studies physically realizable universal adversarial patches, transfer across models/datasets, and print-and-capture effects. Its attacker knows the model during patch optimization, and the recognition endpoint is misclassification on aligned ROIs. The paper explicitly leaves liveness and multimodal authentication outside scope and names richer deployment settings with liveness as future work. This leaves a narrowly bounded end-to-end action audit to test, not a new capture-aware patch algorithm.
- **Physical PAD:** GBU-Palm [K] supplies 21,326 videos from 105 people, six environments, print/replay presentations, RGB/NIR, identity/attack-lineage splits, and environment-held-out evaluation. It reports architecture-dependent shift and no consistent benefit from RGB+NIR fusion. This kills generic RGB/NIR PAD/fusion and environment robustness claims. It does not supply this project's exact live Pi ROI and simulated debit path.
- **Cross-hand identity:** CCPV [K] already trains and evaluates left/right cross-chirality recognition, with one stored palm and the other hand usable at verification, including open-set tests. A "use the other hand" algorithm claim is occupied. A study of whether that fallback actually rescues users in this specific fixed camera/payment interface is only an apparatus-specific usability/action evaluation.
- **Template privacy:** Palmprint template reconstruction is itself an established direct task. Yan et al. [K] (Pattern Recognition 155, 110655, Nov. 2024) report a black-box reconstruction attack on handcrafted and deep templates, while multiple recent palmprint papers propose cancelable/encrypted matching. A generic reconstruction or cancelable-template method claim is occupied. A threat audit of the exact local storage format would be a finite security evaluation, if the implementation stores embeddings and the relevant model/data are available.
- **Bob Zhang adjacency:** PalmBridge and FedPalm are close open-set/representation neighbors; *A Dual-Level Cancelable Framework for Palmprint Verification and Hack-Proof Data Storage* (arXiv:2403.02680v1) has Bob Zhang as a coauthor and directly occupies a broad protected-template method claim. Advisor fit does not establish novelty.

### Pareto comparison

- `PATCH-ACTION` has the clearest harm and a plausible end-to-end endpoint, but the closest new primary paper is extremely direct and the physical/ethics setup is demanding. Its useful result may be a local negative audit.
- `CHIRAL-RESCUE` has a simple user story and modest engineering cost, but the core algorithmic claim is already directly occupied. It is the easiest to demonstrate and the least likely to survive as a research contribution.
- `TEMPLATE-LEAK` has a strong privacy story and avoids new participant collection, but template reconstruction and cancelable protection are established fields. A narrowly scoped audit of the local system could be worthwhile only if its stored representation creates a meaningful threat model.
- `NIR-DROP` addresses a real sensing failure mode, but requires more apparatus and authorization. New X-Palm and GBU-Palm work make generic cross-domain, missing-spectrum, fusion, and PAD claims difficult to defend.

No candidate dominates. `PATCH-ACTION` ranks highest for local story/endpoint distinction; `TEMPLATE-LEAK` ranks highest for software-only feasibility if the app actually stores a reusable embedding. Neither outranks the existing `E-BOUND-X` route without a readiness gate and exact direct-neighbor audit.

## Top two formalizations

### 1. `PATCH-ACTION`: capture-to-action adversarial patch audit

- **[D] Decision:** frozen Palm Payment mock transaction returns `commit / reject / unknown` for one physical palm presentation.
- **[A] Actor:** a deliberately bounded evaluator acting as an attacker who can place a printed visual marking on a consenting participant's palm; this is a controlled research threat model, not a deployment allegation.
- **[T] Task/observations:** full VIS camera frame, frame/capture ID, ROI status, matcher scores/rank, PAD/quality outputs if present, gallery label, and terminal mock-action result. Compare matched presentations with and without a patch.
- **Observable outcome:** difference in wrong identity action, correct-owner completion, reject/unknown, ROI/PAD rejection, and capture-to-action time. Unit of analysis is person, not repeated frame. Keep action outcomes separate from classifier ASR.
- **Counterexample:** if the Pi's ROI or PAD gate blocks the marked sample, or the terminal action never changes across held-out people, the proposed mechanism did not affect the actual decision path.
- **Negative-result value:** identifies a finite capture pipeline that suppresses the known ROI-level attack, or shows that ROI-level attack rates do not translate to action outcomes in this setup.
- **Readiness gate:** obtain authorization; freeze code, gallery, threshold and baseline; atomically bind images to frame/session/decision records; predeclare identities, unknowns, patch geometry/budget, and person-blocked split. Do not use a few zero wrong actions to claim security.

### 2. `TEMPLATE-LEAK`: exact storage-format exposure audit

- **[D] Decision:** security owner chooses whether the local enrollment format is acceptable for prototype storage, should be replaced with a protected baseline, or should retain raw images only transiently.
- **[A] Actor:** database-only attacker and stronger database-plus-model attacker are evaluated as separate capability levels.
- **[T] Task/observations:** local stored files/embeddings, released model and public training palm data; attack attempts identity linkage and reconstruction-to-matcher reauthentication. No new protection transform is proposed.
- **Observable outcome:** linkage AUC/rank, reconstruction similarity, re-match/impersonation success against a held-out gallery, storage/latency cost; report attacker data and compute budget.
- **Counterexample:** if current storage is raw imagery, the proposed embedding attack is irrelevant; if a public nearest-neighbor or simple inversion baseline performs equally, the elaborate audit adds no technical result.
- **Negative-result value:** shows which locally stored fields are sufficient for linkage/re-match under a declared threat model, or that a specific format resists the evaluated attacker only.
- **Readiness gate:** inspect actual persisted artifacts and access boundaries first; determine whether public code/data can train a realistic attacker; confirm data license. Yan et al. Secs. 2-5 could not be fully inspected from the publisher's accessible preview, so their exact attack/evaluation protocol remains a required validation task before any detailed design.

## Decision

HOLD — there are alternative stories worth discussing, but the literature check does not justify promoting a new Palm method. The cleanest new research question among these is a narrowly scoped `PATCH-ACTION` audit that measures whether known physical perturbations survive the actual ROI/PAD/open-set/payment path; its likely contribution is finite evaluation and potentially a useful null. `TEMPLATE-LEAK` is the easiest software-only backup only if code inspection confirms the app stores a reusable feature representation and an explicit attacker is relevant. Cross-chirality and missing NIR are good product questions but collide with direct recognition work or require new optical capability. The validation role should try to kill `PATCH-ACTION` and inspect Yan et al.'s full reconstruction paper before the coordinator changes the active register.

HOLD
