# Divergence Packet: 2026-08-28 fresh low-cost edge directions

## Decision investigated

Independently test whether a direction materially different from locked C3 is ready to replace or complement it under the confirmed constraints: self-purchasable low-cost parts, a runnable benchtop demo by 2026-12, and experimental work in 2027-H1.  No personal data, production deployment, safety actuation, major infrastructure, or expensive instruments are assumed.  The decision is **not** whether an edge demo can be built; it is whether a direction has a bounded decision problem and a claim that a direct baseline can refute.

## Top two formalizations

### Q1: physical camera condition, local count-or-unknown

- [D] For an item passage (x_t), a record operator chooses (a_t \in \{\mathrm{count}(y), \mathrm{unknown}\}\). Wrong retained counts cost one record error; `unknown` consumes a fixed review budget (B\). The scope excludes safety control and identity recognition.
- [A] The device observes a frozen detector label/margin, exposure/blur proxies, and frame timing. It may retain a count or emit `unknown`; it cannot request a cloud prediction or update thresholds on target conditions.
- [T] Select source physical conditions and a finite rule family before target capture. On a held-out camera plus degradation/motion block, test whether the family improves the wrong-retained-count versus review-budget frontier over margin-only, image-quality-only, and periodic-review rules.
- Observable counterexample: all source-tuned combined rules are weakly dominated by a single detector-margin threshold on the held-out block.
- Negative-result value: establishes that additional cheap quality features do not safely transfer; a simple local reject option is sufficient for this finite setup.
- Boundary: arbitrary new physical conditions are not identified. Generalized selective classification already covers distribution shift at the classifier level; therefore the only possible contribution is an audited *physical protocol and boundary*, subject to collision search. [GAP]

### Q2: low-cost asynchronous two-view decision

- [D] For a time-indexed tabletop item state (z_t), a local recorder chooses (a_t \in \{\mathrm{fuse},\mathrm{freshest\ view},\mathrm{unknown}\}\). Wrong retained position/count is an error; `unknown` and latency are bounded costs.
- [A] Each peer supplies acquisition timestamp, receipt timestamp, current frame and a frozen detector output. No perfect shared clock, remote compute or learned feature compensator is allowed.
- [T] On source cells, calibrate timestamp skew intervals and a finite visual-consistency guard. Freeze them. On a held-out jitter/offset/motion cell, compare with timestamp-only max-age and freshest-view policies at matched retained-error and latency budgets.
- Observable counterexample: a timestamp-only interval guard has equal or better frontier, or any visual disagreement is simply explained by target motion rather than staleness.
- Negative-result value: supports the simpler clock/age guard and avoids an unjustified visual-fusion mechanism.
- Boundary: with only two observations, certain object motion and time offset combinations are observationally indistinguishable; universal stale-frame identification is impossible. [C] A formal lower-bound source has not yet been retrieved. [GAP]
