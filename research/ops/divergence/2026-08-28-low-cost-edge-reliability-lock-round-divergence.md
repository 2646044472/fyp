# Divergence Packet: 2026-08-28, evidence-complete edge sensing after outages

## Decision investigated

Find 3--7 genuinely different, low-cost, no-human-data edge sensing/reliability candidates after the user confirmed that affordable components may be self-purchased.  The current D1 finite-family ToF `clear / uncertain` study was treated as a constraint, not a default.  **No candidate in this packet is locked or called novel.**  Every proposed distinction remains **Amber** until component-collision, exact-claim-collision, and boundary/impossibility audits are complete.

The most promising *hypothesis* is R1: a physical, provenance-aware measurement protocol for an offline temperature logger.  It is deliberately a decision/evidence question, not a new anomaly detector, wireless protocol, or food/vaccine safety claim.

**Scope guard:** the proposed bench uses an insulated container and non-hazardous thermal mass only.  It must not claim validation for vaccines, food, clinical samples, real cold-chain regulation, protective interlocks, or autonomous disposal.  The real story is a *laboratory materials custodian* deciding whether a test material's temperature record is sufficient to release it from a simulated custody workflow, or whether it must be inspected/quarantined.  The harm is either an unsupported release after an unobserved excursion, or needless rework/discard of a benign test material.  The operator, decision, and consequences are therefore real but bounded.

### Pareto reading

- **R1** has the strongest bounded actor/decision/harm and a safe, self-collected physical artifact.  Its direct-neighbor risk is high but crisp: its contribution disappears if standard QoS/sequence persistence suffices.  It offers value even when negative by reporting the precise information missing after each fault class.
- **R2** is systems-distinct from R1's thermal inference, but the provenance/event-log neighborhood is more crowded.  It should be treated as R1's separable systems sub-study rather than a second FYP unless validation finds a clear endpoint distinction.
- **R3** has a clean energy/latency metric and easy rig, but overlaps the canonical E selective-observation family.  It is a reserve boundary experiment, not a promotion target.
- **R4** has the cleanest impossibility result but the weakest distinct CS contribution and has inherited D2's self-diagnosis collision risk.  It is useful primarily as R1's fault-model appendix.

## Top two formalizations

### R1: outage-provenance thermal evidence ledger

- **[D]** At nominal sample index `i`, the primary node observes `z_i` (or `read-failed`), local supply/reboot state `b_i`, and local delivery state `a_i`; it persists a bounded record `r_i=(epoch, sequence-range, read-status, b_i, a_i)`.  A separate reference logger observes `y(t)` continuously for evaluation only.  Threshold `tau` and allowable exposure budget `L` are frozen before collection.
- **[A]** Within a declared container/thermal-mass/opening family, a calibrated finite rate envelope `|dy/dt| <= v_c` is valid for each condition cell `c`; persisted provenance is not corrupted by the injected non-adversarial fault.  Both are **[GAP]** before pilot measurement and are not claimed beyond the finite family.
- **[T]** Let `H(O)` be the set of reference histories consistent with a policy's observable record `O`, the explicitly stated rate envelope, and fault model.  Define `E(h)` as time above `tau`.  The policy emits `known-safe` only when `sup_{h in H(O)} E(h) <= L`, `known-violation` only when `inf_{h in H(O)} E(h) > L`, otherwise `indeterminate`.  On object/episode/fault cells held out before collection, the R1 record reduces the rate of reference-labelled benign intervals classified `indeterminate`, without increasing the rate of reference-labelled violating intervals classified `known-safe`, versus all four listed baselines at equal sample/radio budget.
- **Observable outcome:** unsafe known-safe rate; correct violation flag rate; unnecessary indeterminate rate; the distribution of unresolvable gap duration; packet bytes; power; reset recovery latency; and coverage by fault/thermal cell.  The reference trace is scored after, never fed to decisions.
- **Counterexample / boundary:** if the node loses power before persisting any record and the pre/post temperature plus allowed rate envelope admit both a threshold-crossing and non-crossing path, the status cannot honestly be `known-safe` or `known-violation`.  This is a useful result, not a model failure.
- **Negative-result value:** establish that source-local provenance buys no additional correct release decisions over `QoS+sequence` or `any gap => indeterminate`, or publish the exact outage duration/rate/fault cells that cannot be identified with low-cost instrumentation.  Either falsifies an information claim directly.

### R3: event-conditioned bounded missed-excursion duration

- **[D]** At sample `i`, observe `x_i=(z_i, door_i, elapsed_i, battery/energy state_i)`; choose next sampling interval `Delta_i` from a bounded set.  The evaluation reference trace is `y(t)` and is not available to the schedule.
- **[A]** Each finite condition cell has a pre-measured rate envelope after a door/contact event, and door state provides information beyond time elapsed.  These assumptions may fail on unseen thermal mass, airflow, or sensor placement.
- **[T]** At a fixed measured energy budget, an event/slope policy maintains a smaller 95th-percentile or maximum (predeclared) undetected duration above `tau` than the energy-matched periodic policy on held-out condition cells.
- **Observable outcome:** reference-labelled undetected excursion duration, energy per episode, sample count, and failure of the declared rate envelope.
- **Counterexample / boundary:** an unobserved heat source can cause a rate change immediately after a sample with no contact event; then no scheduler using these observations can guarantee the claimed bound.  A continuous or independent sentinel would be required.
- **Negative-result value:** a physical no-gain result says that a simple periodic schedule is Pareto-optimal for the measured container family.  That is more useful than replacing it with another trigger model.
