# Validation Audit: 2026-08-31 six biometric-edge themes, second independent round

## Decision investigated

Whether any of P1 cross-device robustness versus linkability, P2-CTB template-reconstruction transfer, P3 heterogeneous protected templates, P4 feedback-adaptive attacks, P5 uncertainty-aware authentication, or P6 cross-spectral/missing-modality authentication retains a falsifiable FYP research claim after the current 2025--2026 literature and data/protocol audit. Particular attention was given to the active P2 claim: a leaked source template `e_A` is decoded to an image `x_hat`, then accepted by target `B` at a target threshold frozen from an independent FAR set.

## Claim under test

P2-CTB claims a conditional *boundary study*, rather than a new attack: after leakage from device/sensor/model A, measure whether a public-surrogate reconstruction baseline causes acceptance by a heterogeneous target B, split by source/target device, spectrum, and matcher-model cells. The active prospectus calls `x_hat` a reconstructed presentation and treats a null transfer rate as a finite security barrier.

The claim fails if its proposed data/implementation feeds a pre-cropped generated ROI directly to the target matcher, because no target acquisition, ROI localisation, colour/spectral response, capture quality gate, or presentation-attack detector is then in the observation path. It also fails as a distinct research contribution if prior work already has template-to-image reconstruction, cross-algorithm/cross-dataset target matching, and the attack-acceptance endpoint; adding a Pi, a different lightweight matcher, or an unpaired device label is then an evaluation sweep rather than a new action or endpoint.

## Assumption and identification audit

- [KILL] In the prospectus's formal acceptance rule, `f_B(x_hat)` is evaluated on the generated image. That models a digital injection after target capture/ROI preprocessing, not a physical presentation at B. Calling it cross-device, cross-sensor, or a reconstructed *presentation* is unsupported unless `x_hat` is converted to an attack instrument and captured by B under a frozen target preprocessing/PAD pipeline.
- [KILL] X-Palm cannot identify a device effect: only 81 participants have both domains; the smartphone domain varies device, pose, distance, illumination, background, occlusion, text/wet condition and other factors together. The paper's own closed-set protocol isolates a broad domain shift, not individual device/sensor causes. A model-cell result may still be reported as an empirical dataset-domain result, but it cannot prove that a sensor, ROI, or template transform caused success/failure.
- [KILL] A target threshold frozen on bona-fide genuine/impostor pairs gives an ordinary verification operating point, not a presentation-attack evaluation. Without attack-presentation samples, PAD, and APCER/BPCER, target acceptance says only that the matcher accepts a digital input. ISO/IEC 30107-1 terminology is relevant, but the cited standard does not provide a PAD result for this system.
- [KILL] A null rate near the matched FAR has at least four indistinguishable explanations under the planned direct-ROI protocol: insufficient decoder fidelity, source/target ROI/normalisation mismatch, compound dataset-domain mismatch, or target-model robustness. It does not establish a ``finite transfer barrier'' attributable to heterogeneous edge hardware or template protection.
- [KILL] A Raspberry Pi target runtime does not repair either identification failure. It changes latency/RAM only. The existing 2026-08-31 Pi audit also establishes that the available camera/archive route lacks confirmed paired permissions, target-FAR calibration, deterministic live ROI, and PAD.
- [GAP] The X-Palm repository requires a signed academic EULA and gives a time-limited download after approval. There is no evidence yet that its EULA permits a template-inversion/spoofing experiment, even when images are not redistributed. Approval for recognition benchmarking is not automatically permission for attack generation.
- [GAP] No inspected full paper reports the exact all-factor P2 matrix. This is not evidence of novelty, because the proposed matrix changes neither attacker action nor target endpoint from the direct neighbors.

## Strongest simple baseline

For the direct-ROI P2 protocol, the decisive baselines are not a Pi or a more elaborate decoder: (1) feed the original source-domain ROI to target B; (2) feed an arbitrary same-identity bona-fide target ROI as an upper-bound positive control; (3) feed an identity-disjoint surrogate/nearest-neighbour ROI and random bona-fide ROIs; and (4) repeat every condition after the *same frozen B-side preprocessing*. If the reconstruction does not beat (3), it is at the operating-point false-match rate; if it cannot approach (2), the result is a domain/ROI compatibility failure before any template-leak conclusion. For a physical claim, add an unmodified bona-fide print/display-and-recapture control, a no-attack instrument control, and PAD APCER/BPCER; otherwise report ``digital matcher injection'' only.

## Contrarian result

The active P2 framing calls a null result a ``finite transfer barrier,'' but the available X-Palm protocol makes its target input a normalised 112x112 ROI and intentionally confounds smartphone model with acquisition conditions. A negative result therefore lacks the causal identification required to call it a hardware, sensor, or protection barrier; a positive result is already close to the reconstruction/cross-dataset acceptance results of Yan et al. The useful null is a reproduction failure with a fixed public protocol, not a new security boundary.

## Feasibility audit

- **Data:** X-Palm access is gated by a signed EULA and only 81 paired scanner/smartphone participants exist. Its public repository offers code, but not proof that a spoofing/inversion project is authorised. The local archives previously inspected have no demonstrated licence/provenance. [GAP]
- **Labels:** Source/target identity pairs exist only for the 81 paired X-Palm participants. Metadata described in the repository does not create controlled per-phone, per-spectrum, per-ROI-error repetitions. The claimed device × sensor × model matrix therefore contains sparse or confounded cells. [KILL]
- **Attack validity:** Yan's full paper reports GPU training and an image-generation attack; the P2 proposal substitutes a small MLP decoder without evidence that it passes same-device attack acceptance. If it fails, replacing it with an easier model is not a P2 result. [KILL gate]
- **Physical endpoint:** CAAP and EMPalm both make physical/capture assumptions explicit. P2 neither defines an attack instrument nor a B-side capture/PAD protocol. A Pi execution benchmark cannot serve as either. [KILL]
- **Time:** Reproducing Yan, obtaining EULA permission, implementing a controlled physical B capture path, and constructing matched source/target/PAD splits are each substantive projects. The one-year FYP schedule cannot assume all four without a passed data/ethics preflight. [GAP]

## Decision

P1, P3, P4, P5 and P6 remain killed as broad mechanisms. Kill P2-CTB as the active research direction: its direct-ROI protocol neither tests a target device/sensor/presentation pathway nor identifies a security barrier, while its positive reconstruction-to-target-acceptance endpoint is materially occupied by Yan et al. and strengthened by EMPalm/CAAP. Retain only two non-thesis artifacts: a labelled reproduction of template reconstruction under a frozen public direct-ROI protocol, and the existing Pi matcher as an engineering/runtime demonstration after separate data/ethics approval. A future candidate would need a new, independently controlled physical observation/action/endpoint, not a larger P2 transfer matrix.
