# Validation Audit: 2026-08-29 palm/biometric edge directions

## Decision investigated

I audited six supplied directions separately: (1) cross-device robustness jointly constrained by privacy/linkability; (2) transferability of template-reconstruction attacks across devices, sensors and models; (3) protected templates on heterogeneous edge devices measured by EER, unlinkability, revocability and cross-device degradation; (4) adaptive black-box attacks using matcher feedback; (5) quality/uncertainty-aware authentication under unseen devices, low light, blur and open-set conditions; and (6) cross-spectral or missing-modality robustness. The current charter and candidate register were read first; palm/edge overlap was checked against the archived edge-sensing material.

## Claim under test

Each proposal claims a defensible edge-computing contribution rather than a model swap: a measurable security, robustness or decision improvement under a declared device/attack/resource constraint. The null result must remain useful if a matched standard baseline is as good.

## Assumption and identification audit

- [K] Device/domain shift is a documented palmprint problem. Shen et al. (Pattern Recognition 132, 2022) created CrossDevice-A/B; Jia et al. (Pattern Recognition 165, 2025) describes device/environment shifts and single-source DG.
- [K] ISO/IEC 24745:2022 requires protection of biometric information, including irreversibility, unlinkability and renewability/revocability requirements; it is a 63-page International Standard published 2022-02-08. https://www.iso.org/standard/75302.html
- [K] ISO/IEC 29794-1:2024 defines quality-score terms, aggregation and evaluation but does not standardize a quality algorithm or minimum score. https://www.iso.org/standard/79519.html
- [C] Cross-device EER differences can be decomposed into identity, device, session and illumination effects only with paired identities and a blocked device/session design; public datasets often do not have all factors crossed.
- [KILL] A protected-template method cannot claim irreversibility from visual inspection or entropy alone. ISO threat models and inversion/similarity attacks must be specified; Ali et al. show that removing LSH leaves highly separable mated/non-mated distributions (D_link=0.98) while the full transform reports 0.01.
- [KILL] Unlabeled uncertainty cannot certify authentication risk under unseen shifts without assumptions or an audit arm. A blur/low-light score may be correlated with nuisance while the identity-label mechanism changes.
- [C] Transfer attack success is not a pure security property when target and source sensors use different ROI extraction, spectra, normalization or quantization; those transformations can make a physically good reconstruction unmatchable.

## Strongest simple baseline

For candidates 1, 3, 5 and 6, a fixed ArcFace/CCNet (or PalmBridge where applicable) with standard score calibration, quality threshold and abstain-on-low-quality policy is the decisive baseline. For candidate 2, evaluate Yan et al.'s released/protocol-equivalent ProGAN attack and a direct pixel/latent optimization attack with identical query and compute budgets. For candidate 4, hard-label random search and score-based boundary attack with query-rate limiting are required. Any claimed gain must survive held-out device/sensor/model cells and report EER/FAR/FRR, attack success, query count, latency, energy and privacy metrics.

## Contrarian result

The common “edge” argument does not create novelty: running an existing matcher or protection transform on a phone/SBC changes deployment cost, not task, observation model or guarantee. A one-year undergraduate project also cannot credibly validate global privacy claims, because irreversibility and unlinkability are threat-model properties and require attack implementations, key management and cross-application data. The only potentially defensible residual is a negative/boundary experiment: under a fixed low-cost device set and explicit budgets, measure where cross-device transfer or missing-modality decisions become indistinguishable and when abstention dominates learned adaptation.

## Feasibility audit

- **Data:** PolyU, IITD, Tongji and X-Palm are public, but paired identities across all target sensors and modalities are not guaranteed; collecting new palms requires consent/ethics, which the charter disallows for personal data.
- **Hardware:** heterogeneous edge claims need at least two camera/spectral devices, stable illumination control, and power/latency instrumentation. EM side-channel work needs RF probes and synchronized capture, beyond a typical FYP budget.
- **Compute:** Yan/ProGAN reconstruction reports roughly one day on an RTX 3090 for training; reproducing attack variants on an edge board is infeasible without offline GPU access.
- **Security evaluation:** ISO unlinkability/irreversibility require attack models, independent keys/tokens and cross-application templates; reporting EER alone is insufficient.
- **Labels:** low-light/blur/open-set quality studies need a held-out degradation protocol and calibration labels; uncertainty without labels is not identifiable.
- **Ethics:** no live authentication, production service or biometric collection is permitted by the charter; use public, de-identified datasets and synthetic edge faults only.

Failed/uncertain searches: no primary paper was found that evaluates all three axes (cross-device EER, ISO unlinkability and revocability) on heterogeneous edge hardware; no full device × sensor × model transfer matrix for palm reconstruction was located; no standard defines a palm-specific uncertainty score for open-set authentication. These absences are [GAP], not novelty evidence.

## Decision

1. Cross-device robustness + privacy/linkability: **KILL** as a combined algorithm claim; possible protocol-only pivot with explicit resource and ISO threat model.
2. Reconstruction transfer across devices/sensors/models: **HOLD** for a narrow, preregistered transfer/indistinguishability boundary; kill if it only reimplements Yan et al. or lacks multiple sensors.
3. Protected templates on heterogeneous edge devices: **KILL** broad scheme claim; only a systems-cost replication remains.
4. Adaptive black-box matcher-feedback attacks: **KILL**; endpoint and feedback threat are established.
5. Quality/uncertainty-aware unseen-device authentication: **KILL** generic method; retain only a calibration/abstention boundary study.
6. Cross-spectral/missing-modality robustness: **KILL** generic method; **PIVOT** to edge-budgeted acquisition/abstention with an explicit missingness mechanism.

Overall validation outcome: **PIVOT**
