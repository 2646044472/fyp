# Validation Audit: 2026-10-05 HKSL Recognition on Edge

## Decision investigated

**HOLD.** Investigate whether “Cantonese sign-language recognition on edge” is a defensible new FYP direction. I interpret “Cantonese sign language” provisionally as Hong Kong Sign Language (HKSL); it must not be treated as Cantonese speech translated word-for-word into signs. The idea is socially meaningful and locally grounded, but edge deployment and HKSL recognition each have substantial direct neighbors. It needs a narrower decision and a confirmed lawful data path before it can be promoted.

## Claim under test

Candidate claim: for a defined HKSL task, an on-device recognizer can preserve useful recognition and response quality under edge resource limits while handling signers or lexical variants excluded from training. A defensible contribution would have to specify whether the input is an isolated dictionary sign or a continuous conversation, which output is judged (gloss, Cantonese text, or a user-facing action), the target hardware, the held-out signer/variant protocol, and a measurable error-cost endpoint. “Run an HKSL model on a Raspberry Pi” alone is a deployment demonstration, not the research distinction.

## Assumption and identification audit

- **Language definition [K/GAP]:** HKSL is a sign language with its own lexicon, grammar and variation. HKU's lexical database provides translations into English and Hong Kong Cantonese; this does not make HKSL “Cantonese speech in visual form.” The prompt’s wording could mean HKSL, a Cantonese mouthing/signing task, or another signed variety. Those imply different labels and tasks.
- **Task choice [GAP]:** Isolated-sign recognition and continuous sign-language recognition (CSLR) are different tasks. HKSLLEX supplies dictionary/citation signs performed in isolation. TVB-HKSL-News and the SignBERT/HKU-Portable corpora address continuous data. A model tested on pre-segmented clips does not demonstrate streaming recognition or sentence boundary detection.
- **Spatial-temporal observations [K]:** Signs may depend on handshape, location, movement, two-hand relation and non-manual signals. RTG-Net explicitly extracts face/hand/body regions and correlations across adjacent frames; an edge claim based only on hand crops or static poses risks discarding the observation needed to distinguish signs. HKSL lexical resources document handshape and morpheme segmentation, and corpus transcriptions include non-manual/linguistic structure.
- **Language output [GAP]:** Gloss sequence accuracy is not Cantonese sentence translation. HKSL and spoken Cantonese need not share word order or lexical segmentation. If the story is access to services or communication, the endpoint must be judged with Deaf/HKSL users, not inferred from frame-level accuracy.
- **Signer and variant generalization [K/GAP]:** TVB-HKSL-News has only two signers and is explicitly signer-dependent. SignBERT's lab dataset has six signers. HKSL Browser records lexical variants across Deaf organizations and reports age/organization metadata. A random video split is not evidence of signer-independent or regional/community-variant performance. Distinct organizations and demographic labels may be unavailable in research corpora.
- **Leakage controls [C]:** Split by signer first, then session/source program and sentence; deduplicate adjacent clips/takes; report vocabulary overlap and class support for each split. If the goal includes variants, reserve a documented variant source/community for test and preserve variant-aware labels. Hyperparameter selection must not inspect that test set. With only two or six signers, signer-held-out results will have wide uncertainty and limited population coverage.
- **Edge interpretation [K]:** RTG-Net calls a Surface Pro 6 (i7-8650U, 16 GB RAM) an edge device and reports time/resource metrics. A Raspberry Pi is a stricter target but does not itself create novelty. Separate capture, preprocessing/keypoint extraction, model, and end-to-end latency; report memory, power/energy, thermal throttling, and sustained throughput rather than only model inference.

## Strongest simple baseline

For a bounded edge study, use an established compact video/sign baseline (e.g., RTG-Net or TinyMSLR where task-compatible) and a simple MobileNet/2D-CNN plus temporal pooling/GRU baseline, then export and profile those same weights on the target board. Compare with the same model on a laptop or GPU. A person/variant-blocked evaluation plus ordinary quantization or a standard compact model is the baseline any proposed model contribution must beat. For the application, use an explicit abstain/repeat/ask-human output and report coverage-versus-error; a forced top-1 answer is an unfair baseline for language ambiguity.

## Contrarian result

The compelling part is the low-resource local language and user group; the weak part is the proposed technical claim. “HKSL on edge” combines an already-studied language-recognition task with an already-studied edge-deployment constraint. If the project only fine-tunes an existing model on public examples and reports accuracy/latency, it is a useful prototype but likely not a defensible FYP research contribution. The best remaining route is an explicitly co-designed evaluation of signer/lexical-variant robustness under a constrained on-device interaction, after data rights and participant recruitment are confirmed.

## Feasibility audit

- **Data availability [K/GAP]:** HKSL Corpus includes naturalistic, elicited and experimental videos, but some content is privacy-protected and access requires a request and consent. HKSLLEX appears browseable/downloadable, with video and lexicon files, but its data license, signer repetition counts, consent scope for ML training, and machine-ready train/test split were not verified. The HKSL Browser is an important variant catalog, not automatically an ML training corpus.
- **TVB dataset rights [KILL/GAP]:** The current official request-form v2 specifies that the license expired on 2025-09-14 and forbids sharing, exhibiting, publishing or editing material without written permission. As of this audit (2026-10-05), do not assume a new FYP can train/evaluate on it; obtain a replacement written license directly from HKUST/TVB before relying on it.
- **Human participants [GAP]:** The proposed generalization claim needs multiple Deaf/HKSL signers, repeated sessions and Deaf/HKSL expert annotation or review, plus appropriate consent/ethics approval. Availability of participants, expert time, compensation budget and approval timeline is unconfirmed. Convenient classmates signing a short vocabulary would test a constructed gesture recognizer, not HKSL usability.
- **Labels and target [GAP]:** Gloss labels can differ from translations; lexical alternatives may be community- or age-associated. Must decide whether accepted variants map to one concept label, remain distinct output variants, or trigger clarification. Need a Deaf-led label policy and adjudication for ambiguous/non-manual cases.
- **Hardware [GAP]:** Pi 5 or similar can likely host an inference path, but camera capture, hand/face keypoints, video decode, thermal load, power and continuous-session latency are unmeasured. There is no confirmed edge accelerator, sensor, or compute budget in the prompt.
- **Time and scope [K/GAP]:** Workspace charter gives a one-year FYP and Dec 2026 demo target (local project constraint; current date Oct 2026). A corpus-permission and ethics gate could dominate the remaining demo window. A smaller isolated-sign pilot may be achievable but has less realistic conversational value and direct model collisions.
- **User value [GAP]:** Recognition can be inaccurate or misleading; the project should not promise automatic translation for consequential service interactions. Define a low-risk co-design scenario and retain human correction/abstention.

## Decision

**HOLD.** Do not promote “HKSL recognition on edge” as a method claim. Continue only after the project owner clarifies the language/task and secures a lawful dataset plus Deaf/HKSL signer and annotation access. A viable next candidate would be a bounded signer- and lexical-variant-held-out HKSL evaluation on a named Pi-class device, with an explicit abstain/correction endpoint and an edge-resource frontier. That candidate still requires a direct follow-up search for the exact variant-generalization claim and a pilot showing enough independent signers/variants to estimate it.

HOLD
