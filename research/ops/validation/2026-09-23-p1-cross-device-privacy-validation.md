# Validation: P1 cross-device palm robustness versus cross-service linkability

## Decision investigated

The 2026-08-29 P1 formulation proposed a monotone cross-device FNMR versus cross-service linkability frontier. The newly available `research/ops/divergence/2026-09-23-p1-cross-device-privacy-divergence.md` explicitly withdraws monotonicity and offers five variants: P1-A `FRONTIER-AUDIT`, P1-B `KEY-CROSSOVER`, P1-C `DEVICE-NORMALIZATION-LEAK`, P1-D `PARETO-NEGATIVE`, and P1-E `EDGE-COST-FRONTIER`. This audit tests those variants and the old positive claim. I read `research/active/README.md`, `00-project-charter.md`, `01-candidate-register.md`, both P1 divergence packets and `research/ops/validation/2026-08-29-palm-biometric-edge-audit.md`.

**Result:** KILL the positive algorithm/monotone-frontier claim; PIVOT A/D only to a data- and threat-model-gated finite audit/negative boundary. B/C are HOLD as bounded attack-condition checks; E is engineering. The current Pi 5 Palm Payment project is a one-service, local, identifier-free VIS transaction prototype, not an authorized cross-service privacy study.

## Attacker and identification boundary

P1 needs a concrete confidentiality owner and attacker: are two relying services colluding, or has one service's template/key leaked? Does the attacker obtain protected templates, similarity scores, metadata, query access, auxiliary palm images, or multiple records per identity? Does the key remain secret after compromise? Cross-service linking of the *same person* and same-service verification are different endpoints. A token-independent score-only threat and a known-key multi-score threat cannot be collapsed into one privacy number.

The “increasing device-invariant representation” intervention is undefined. Alignment weight, embedding dimension, quantization, extractor capacity, and protection strength vary different mechanisms and costs. A monotone causal curve is not identifiable without a fixed intervention family, repeated independent keys, paired identities, frozen operating points and the same population in utility and attack trials. A plotted nondominated set over selected configurations is only a finite empirical surface; it does not establish a general robustness-privacy law.

| Factor | Required control |
| --- | --- |
| Person and gallery | Same identities on each device; identity-disjoint development/test; fixed mate-absent roster. |
| Device versus service | Factorial device-by-service design; independently randomized service keys; no device-service confounding. |
| Session/illumination | Repeated physical sessions and blocked conditions; avoid same-image train/test pairs. |
| Verification endpoint | FMR/FNMR at pre-frozen thresholds, including held-out device and unknown users. |
| Linkability endpoint | Predeclared attacker tiers, maximal linkability or cross-service reidentification with multi-score composition and uncertainty. |
| Edge budget | Count template size, key/storage, matching CPU/latency, energy and any server/synchronization work. |

## Data and permission feasibility

- [GAP] The current project has no documented legal access to a person-matched, two-device, multi-session palm dataset with sufficiently many independent identities and service-use permission. The old archive's PalmMatchDB card has only a `train` split with insufficient camera/session metadata (`research/archive/2026-08-edge-sensing/log/13-b0-reproducibility-log.md`); it cannot be randomized into a cross-device study. The MPW-180 paper's data availability and actually downloadable artifact were inconsistent in the archive audit (`research/archive/2026-08-edge-sensing/core/05-evidence-ledger.md`, E-41); this is historical negative evidence, not a present-day availability claim.
- [GAP] A public collection's image license is not automatically consent for cross-service reidentification/attack analysis. Before any processing, verify current license, subject consent/IRB or equivalent approval, allowed template derivation and sharing, and deletion policy. This is especially important for a small locally collected cohort.
- [KILL] A two-camera same-session pilot with a handful of volunteers cannot support a rare-error security claim or population-level linkability guarantee. Repeated frames of the same person are dependent observations; image-pair counts do not replace independent identity counts.
- [C] The only feasible bounded design uses an authorized identity-by-device-by-session table, independent simulated service keys, a frozen extractor and matcher, and a preregistered attacker with permitted score/template access. The exact identity/sample requirement must follow a precision/power calculation for the chosen operating point; it cannot be filled in from arbitrary image-pair counts.

## Minimum falsification gate

1. Document the service owner, attacker vantage, exposed artifacts, auxiliary data, key-compromise state and cross-service harm. If no real confidentiality owner is identified, stop.
2. Verify dataset license and crossed identity/device/session metadata before using any biometric samples. If no lawful crossed dataset exists, stop.
3. Freeze extractor, ROI, gallery, device-held-out split, verification thresholds, transform/key generation and independent service-key draws.
4. Run unprotected, device-aligned and per-service protected baselines, including the published Ali-style cancelable construction where reproducible. Treat naive salting as a control, not a security guarantee.
5. Measure FMR/FNMR at frozen operating points and attacker cross-service linkability under one- and multi-score access, key compromise and renewed templates. Include confidence intervals across *people*, not just pairs.
6. Charge template size, CPU/latency, energy and any service overhead. Report the finite nondominated set; compare against the strongest protected and unprotected controls at matched cost.

**Kill result:** if a published cancelable baseline dominates, the privacy endpoint is unmeasurable under the real attacker, or device and service effects cannot be separated, stop the positive claim and report only a bounded replication/negative boundary.

## Decision

**KILL** the original positive combined algorithm or monotone privacy-robustness-frontier thesis. The new divergence A/D protocol and negative boundary are **PIVOT**, B/C are **HOLD** pending a real service/key/metadata threat and crossed data, and E is **KILL as a standalone thesis**. A/D can only be investigated after a lawful crossed dataset, realistic two-service attacker, Gomez-Barrero/multi-score linkage protocol, published cancelable baseline and matched edge-cost measurements exist. The current Palm Payment MVP establishes none of these prerequisites. No P1 variant is promoted.
