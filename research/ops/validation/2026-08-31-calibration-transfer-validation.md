# Validation Audit: 2026-08-31 Cross-device / Cross-illumination Transfer Candidates

## Decision investigated

Audit the divergence packet candidates XFER-ACT, PANEL-GATE, LUX-VOI, CAL-SCHED, and REAL-DIG-RANK for direct-neighbor collision, exact-claim overlap, identifiability, simple-baseline dominance, and Raspberry Pi 5 feasibility. The audit treats the proposed RGB Camera Module / NoIR pair as a finite physical testbed, not as evidence for a population-level camera-transfer guarantee.

## Claim under test

The candidates claim that a low-cost edge node can make a better retain/reacquire/calibrate decision after camera or illumination changes by using (i) a source-device policy transferred to a held-out RGB/NoIR device, (ii) a small printed calibration panel, (iii) an external illuminance (lux) witness, (iv) sequential panel-triggered recalibration, or (v) paired synthetic/physical corruption rank comparisons. A thesis-level claim requires a distinct decision endpoint and a repeatable held-out improvement over fixed two-shot, scalar image-quality, standard-chart, and periodic controls.

## Assumption and identification audit

XFER-ACT assumes that a policy calibrated on one optical path can preserve the ordering of RGB-only, NoIR+IR, and two-shot actions on a second path. With only two purchased cameras, this is a finite paired-device observation, not evidence about an unseen device family. The RGB and NoIR units also differ by an IR-cut filter, so “device swap” and “modality change” are confounded. A valid experiment must freeze exposure/white balance, use the same static target and fixture, and obtain the exact payload from an independent flatbed/phone oracle. The two-world test is mandatory: construct matched visible previews where one world is transparent-cover glare and the other is low visible light; if the observations available before the action are indistinguishable but the best actions differ, no policy can certify the decision.

PANEL-GATE assumes a small patch panel is a sufficient statistic for scene-level decode risk. This fails under a simple geometry counterexample: the panel has diffuse matte patches while the marker is behind a transparent film at a different angle. A panel can correctly report camera/lamp color while giving no information about the marker's local specular reflection, focus, or print microstructure. A gray/white patch, global brightness, fixed per-device threshold, and always-reacquire policy are therefore mandatory controls. Any positive panel result must be restricted to the declared fixture and material lot.

LUX-VOI assumes an inexpensive BH1750-like illuminance channel adds information beyond image-only quality. CIE's official definition shows lux is a photopic weighting of spectral irradiance; it is not an NIR/radiometric measurement. Equal photopic lux from a warm LED and an 850-nm-rich source can produce the same lux but very different NoIR signal, while an RGB preview already contains scene-dependent brightness and saturation. Thus a lux channel cannot identify which optical path is best in general. To test a narrow residual, lamps must be matched in lux but varied in spectrum, and the image-only policy must receive the same preview, exposure metadata, and timing.

CAL-SCHED assumes reference-panel health predicts marker risk over time and that a sequential policy can beat periodic checks. This is a maintenance scheduling hypothesis, not a new camera mechanism. Focus drift, film placement, and marker geometry can change independently of a panel. Without an independent marker oracle in every episode, the trigger learns only panel repeatability. A positive result must beat periodic, every-frame, and one-quality-threshold schedules at matched false-retain and panel-capture cost; otherwise it is instrumentation.

REAL-DIG-RANK assumes digital corruption severity preserves the ordering of physical faults. Agnihotri et al. directly show that aggregate synthetic/real correlations can be high while per-corruption correlations are weak (Pearson 0.270 for brightness versus night and 0.349 for fog versus fog in Sec. 4.2). The proposed marker version therefore needs a pre-registered finite rank-inversion test and cannot infer universal synthetic invalidity. If the physical cells are chosen after seeing rankings, the test has circularity and no evidentiary value.

## Strongest simple baseline

For XFER-ACT and LUX-VOI, a fixed RGB-then-NoIR+IR two-shot policy with a frozen exact decoder is the dominant control: it removes the policy's opportunity to choose a wrong first action at the cost of one extra capture. Compare against always RGB, always NoIR+IR, brightness/blur/IR-ratio thresholds, random action, and an oracle. If two-shot has a lower false-retain frontier at the same practical energy budget, the adaptive claim is unnecessary.

For PANEL-GATE, use a gray/white patch plus global brightness and a fixed per-device threshold. For CAL-SCHED, use every-frame, fixed-period, and one-quality-threshold schedules. For REAL-DIG-RANK, use direct brightness, blur, saturation, and zero-mask transformations with no learned model. These controls are cheaper, easier to audit, and likely to match any small-panel or lux policy on a static marker fixture.

## Contrarian result

The strongest objection is that the proposed distinctions are endpoint relabeling: “action ranking,” “calibration sufficiency,” and “lux value” can all be implemented as ordinary camera quality control, while the only genuinely new ingredient (an RGB/NoIR pair) is a purchased hardware difference. CCMNet, Lens, Moravec & Šára, and Ma et al. collectively make this objection credible. A positive finite pilot would therefore be most valuable as a boundary/negative result, not evidence of a new adaptive-sensing mechanism.

## Feasibility audit

The physical setup is feasible: Pi 5, RGB and NoIR cameras, IR illuminator, rigid mount, printed QR/AprilTag, opaque/transparent covers, several lamps, and a flatbed/phone oracle are sufficient for a static finite matrix. A lux board and USB power meter are inexpensive, but a lux board must not be treated as a spectral witness. Pi CPU can run decoding and scalar policies; no large training run is required.

The scientific risks are larger than the engineering risks. Only two optical devices provide no statistically defensible population-transfer claim. Sequential Pi camera capture lacks guaranteed synchronized 3A, so moving-target or temporal claims are invalid. Panel and lux labels are not independent scene truth. The experiment must log manual exposure/white balance, lux, lamp identity, intervention state, latency, joules, and exact oracle code; held-out lamp/material/device cells must be blocked before policy tuning. No personal data or safety actuation is needed.

## Decision

- **XFER-ACT: PIVOT (Amber), not PROMOTE.** A finite RGB/NoIR action-rank table can be useful, but CCMNet and Lens occupy cross-device/adaptive control components. Promote only a preregistered held-out rank inversion or a useful null; no transfer guarantee.
- **PANEL-GATE: KILL as a thesis; retain optional preflight.** Chart/color-calibration work and Punnappurath's material limitation collide, and a panel cannot identify local marker glare/focus without an independent scene witness.
- **LUX-VOI: KILL as stated; possible instrumentation-only pivot.** Photopic lux is not spectral/NIR information, and image brightness plus fixed two-shot controls are strong. Only a narrowly matched-spectrum experiment could establish a bounded negative result.
- **CAL-SCHED: KILL as a thesis; optional maintenance characterization.** Online calibration monitoring and recalibration scheduling directly occupy the action and error endpoint; a Pi schedule changes apparatus, not mechanism.
- **REAL-DIG-RANK: PIVOT (negative benchmark).** Agnihotri/Liao/MultiCorrupt/MSC-Bench occupy the broad proxy question. A finite physical-vs-digital marker rank audit is feasible and scientifically useful only as a bounded result.

**Overall decision: HOLD only for XFER-ACT or REAL-DIG-RANK as finite, pre-registered boundary studies; do not purchase a lux sensor for a thesis claim until a matched-spectrum pilot demonstrates incremental information. No candidate is promoted or locked.**
