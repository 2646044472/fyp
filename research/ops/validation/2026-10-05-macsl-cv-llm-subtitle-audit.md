# Validation Audit: 2026-10-05 Macau Sign Language to Cantonese Subtitles with CV+LLM

## Decision investigated

The supplied candidate: use computer vision plus an LLM on an edge device to translate Macau Sign Language (MacSL) video into Cantonese subtitles. This audit tests the whole proposed research claim, not whether a prototype can be assembled.

## Claim under test

Provisional claim: “An edge CV+LLM system translates continuous MacSL video into Macau Cantonese subtitles, with a novel contribution in combining sign recognition and language generation for a low-resource local language.”

[K] The task has a meaningful community-facing application. [KILL] “CV + LLM,” translation of sign video into spoken-language text, and context-aware LLM translation are already direct research families. Replacing Chinese Sign Language with MacSL and putting a model on a Pi is not by itself a distinct CS contribution. The supplied wording does not specify an action, guarantee, constraint beyond hardware, or evaluation endpoint that survives direct work.

Terminology correction: MacSL (澳門手語) is a signed language. It is not spoken Cantonese (澳門粵語). Translation into written Cantonese subtitles requires a target-text convention and fluent Deaf/community review; “Chinese translation”/gloss labels in a dictionary are not automatically spoken-Cantonese subtitle references.

## Assumption and identification audit

- [K] Macao government describes a new Macao Sign Language Vocabulary Bank with video demonstrations, common signs and lexical variants, including use across age groups. It does not describe a continuous conversational corpus, sentence-level paired subtitles, a count of videos/signers, or an open ML license in the announcement.
- [K] The Macau Deaf Association’s public MacSL site describes itself as an online database for common signs and variants; each entry has a Chinese translation “closest to the original sign meaning.” It says the site is maintained/updated and credits Deaf contributors. The page does not grant an ML-training or redistribution license. A lexical demonstration plus a Chinese gloss/meaning is not a gold Cantonese sentence subtitle.
- [K] An older ELAR documentation collection is described by its principal investigator’s CUHK page as 500 target lexical signs from 14 informants, about one hour of narrative, and about 5.5 hours of conversation with partial transcription. ELAR’s 2019 description reports around 6,500 minutes from 16 signers, mixed lexical/narrative/personal-account material, “most” accessible to registered users. Those descriptions are not evidence that all videos are paired with complete, aligned Cantonese subtitle references or cleared for model training/re-release.
- [GAP] Exact file-level access level, license, consent scope for ML training, derivative models, video display, and research re-release for the MacSL ELAR deposit were not verified. Registration/access to view is not permission to train or redistribute. The 2025 official Vocabulary Bank’s actual entry count, signer IDs/count, split-friendly metadata, downloadable files, terms, and adaptation permissions remain unverified.
- [KILL] A dictionary-only system could demonstrate isolated-sign classification or retrieval, but cannot support “continuous video → Cantonese subtitles” without new sentence-level data and independent reference translation. An LLM that expands glosses into fluent text does not recover omitted visual meaning; it can guess plausible content.
- [GAP] “Cantonese subtitles” is underspecified: verbatim colloquial Cantonese in traditional characters, polished written Chinese, or a gloss-faithful semantic paraphrase are different labels. WER/BLEU against one reference may penalize valid variants or reward fluent hallucinations. Require signer-held-out data, multiple acceptable references or Deaf reviewer judgments, meaning/omission/addition error measures, and clear subtitle segmentation/timing.
- [C] A video-only CV+LLM can use facial grammar and hand/body motion; but on-camera recording conditions, coarticulation, signer age, lexical variants and non-manual cues may be underrepresented by dictionary videos. This must be measured rather than assumed.
- [K] SCOPE’s public source says its dataset has 59,231 videos, 72.4 hours, 12 signers, a 5k vocabulary, gloss/text/dialogue labels, and laboratory source. That is a substantially more developed language-resource pipeline than what public MacSL descriptions currently establish. Even for that dataset, signer count is finite; paper split strategy and signer independence must be checked before borrowing its reported scores as a target baseline.

## Strongest simple baseline

If the vocabulary bank provides clips and labels, start with a frozen pose/hand landmark feature extractor plus a linear/nearest-neighbour classifier for a small isolated-sign subset, evaluated with signer-held-out splits and per-variant labels. Compare it with dictionary retrieval and a no-LLM baseline. For sentence translation, compare a gloss-to-text translator using human-verified glosses against the proposed CV-to-gloss-to-LLM pipeline and an end-to-end model. For subtitle safety, the strongest baseline is “show the recognized gloss/uncertainty and ask a human,” not unconditional fluent LLM generation. Until that baseline can be beaten on semantic preservation, the LLM may increase user risk without adding grounded information.

## Contrarian result

[KILL] The proposed positive thesis “edge CV+LLM Macau Sign Language to Cantonese subtitles” is too broad and collides directly with established gloss-free and context-aware SLT. The local language choice is meaningful, but application/domain substitution alone is insufficient. “Edge” has no specified constraint or action beyond running it on a device. The public MacSL Vocabulary Bank is a lexicon/learning resource, not evidence of a continuous video-plus-Cantonese-subtitle corpus. Existing MacSL documentation is promising but has partial transcription and access/rights uncertainty. Thus the current subtitle task is not presently identified or reproducibly evaluable.

[PIVOT] The only plausible next research gate is not yet a model build: obtain written data/ML-use permission from the Vocabulary Bank/IAS or ELAR depositor and Macau Deaf community partners; inventory clip, signer, variant, gloss, transcription, subtitle and consent metadata; then run a small signer-held-out feasibility audit. If only dictionary clips are available, narrow the task to variant-aware isolated-sign recognition/retrieval with community-approved labels. Do not call that subtitle translation. A research contribution would still need a refutable distinction from standard classification/adaptation, such as a prespecified abstain/clarify action for age- or signer-dependent lexical variants at an agreed coverage/error tradeoff; that mechanism is itself subject to direct-neighbor search.

## Feasibility audit

- Data: [GAP] public material is browseable, but rights for computational training, creating derived labels, redistributing clips, or releasing model weights are unverified. Require written confirmation before any model development with protected signers’ videos.
- Scale/labels: [K] The older archive offers nontrivial hours but only 14 informants in its PI summary, 16 signers in ELAR’s summary, and partial transcription. The modern bank’s size and signer-level metadata are not publicly established in the inspected government announcement. A count of 6,350 hearing-impaired residents is not a MacSL signer count.
- Community validity: [GAP] Need Deaf/MacSL-fluent partners to define target written Cantonese, accepted lexical variants, valid subtitle translations, and adjudicate errors. Professional interpreter/community feedback should be part of labels and evaluation.
- Evaluation: [KILL] Random clip split would overstate generalization when repeated recordings share signers, backgrounds, demonstrations, or near-duplicate vocabulary items. Need person-disjoint split and, if evaluating variants, a held-out variant/age cell; quantify uncertainty by signer, not by clip alone.
- LLM faithfulness: [K] SignLLM and SCOPE show the combination exists. Hamidullah et al. (2026 preprint) specifically finds that language priors can produce fluent but video-unsupported SLT output and tests visual counterfactuals. Any claim of useful subtitles needs missing/addition/semantic fidelity review, not only BLEU/ROUGE or subjective fluency.
- Edge feasibility: [GAP] Neither proposal nor local corpus description names a board, frame rate/resolution, end-to-end latency/power budget, on-device LLM, network policy, or resource comparison. CV front end, visual language model and LLM decoding may have very different compute/memory needs. A cloud API would not establish edge inference. Fixed model/hardware profile and measured full-path latency, energy and quality are needed if edge remains a constraint.
- Scope: [K] Current active charter and README lock the project to the existing palmprint direction pending coordinator/user change. This supplied audit does not authorize an active-direction edit.

## Decision

The generic edge CV+LLM translation thesis does not survive direct-neighbor audit. Current data/label/permission descriptions also do not identify a reproducible continuous subtitle task. Keep MacSL as a potentially valuable application, but do not promote the system proposal yet. Proceed only to a data-rights and signer-held-out feasibility gate; if the accessible data are dictionary-only, pivot to a bounded isolated-sign/variant task with Deaf-community labels and a new direct-neighbor audit.
