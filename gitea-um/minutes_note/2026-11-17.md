# FYP Meeting Minutes | 17 November 2026

**Project:** Biometric Palm Recognition

## Report presented: datasets, palm-region extraction and recognition

The meeting focused on the palmprint experiment report. It compared how different systems find a palm region in a photograph and whether that crop lets the recognizer identify the enrolled hand.

- **What data were available?** The report distinguished published dataset size from the images available locally. X-Palm includes phone and scanner views; MPD/PalmSet includes multiple phones and sessions; BJTU provides pre-cropped regions. Public99 derives from 11KHands and is not an independent second population.
- **What was compared?** PalmBridge, SF2Net and PPNet were evaluated with reference or author crops, PKLNet-v5 crops, 21-point letterbox localization and five-view RTM, where the relevant combination was available. The tables reported the correct hand ranked first (rank-1), the number of rankable queries and equal error rate (EER).
- **What did the results show?** On 400 cross-stage BJTU probes with author crops, PalmBridge ranked the correct hand first 366 times, SF2Net 380 and PPNet 327. Their EERs were 7.40%, 8.00% and 9.38% respectively. The best rank-1 count was not the lowest EER. On Tongji, the PKLNet/PalmBridge and five-view RTM/PalmBridge arms had different rankable denominators (5,877 and 5,998); their results are not a paired comparison of identical probes.
- **Where did the pipeline fail?** The examples separated four cases: no usable palm region; a crop that shifts or rotates onto different texture; a wrong identity that is accepted; and a correct top-ranked hand that is nevertheless rejected. Counting returned crops alone would miss the latter three problems.

## Plan for the following week

1. **Compare white-background cutouts with original images.** Use the same photographs for both localization routes and crop recognition texture from the originals. Track usable crops and identity outcomes separately.
2. **Optimize the palm region of interest (ROI).** Check its center, size and orientation across changes in pose and distance. Determine whether repeat captures include the same palm texture.
3. **Try video capture.** Compare a short sequence with a single still to see whether the palm region is more stable.

These three items were next-week plans, not completed experiments presented on 17 November.
