## 1. Progress Update and Demo Results

The team presented the current implementation and offline evaluation results.

### 1.1 Current Recognition Pipeline

- The offline pipeline detects the palm using PKLNet, normalizes the region of interest (ROI) to a 128 × 128 grayscale image, and ranks candidate identities using either DoN or FastCC.
- For the normal-distance cohort, DoN achieved 90.9% Rank-1 accuracy (50/55 queries), while FastCC achieved 89.1% (49/55 queries). The sample size is too small to conclude that either algorithm performs better.
- For the far-distance cohort, Rank-1 accuracy dropped to 40% for DoN (2/5 queries) and 0% for FastCC. Additionally, approximately half of the palm crops failed the geometric validation check.
- The Raspberry Pi's default service currently captures and saves images only. Recognition is available through an optional supervised demo workflow. Therefore, the offline evaluation results should not be presented as real-time performance on the Raspberry Pi.

### 1.2 Immediate Next Steps

- Improve ROI quality checks and geometric validation.
- Calibrate rejection thresholds using independent evaluation data.
- Record capture distances systematically.
- Expand testing across different individuals and capture sessions.

## 2. Potential Research Direction: PalmIR on Edge Devices

The team discussed reproducing PalmIR and exploring its deployment on edge devices.

- **Reproduction:** Establish a working implementation that reproduces the reported PalmIR results.
- **Edge deployment:** Adapt the pipeline for edge devices and evaluate its practical performance.
- **Evaluation metrics:** Measure Equal Error Rate (EER) and False Match Rate (FMR), and investigate whether recognition performance degrades when deployed on resource-constrained devices.
- **Model robustness:** Examine whether the recognition network (RN) encounters performance or compatibility issues during edge deployment.

The research direction should balance recognition accuracy, computational efficiency, and practical deployment constraints.

## 3. Discussion and Action Items

### 3.1 Demo Baseline and Capture Reliability

The immediate priority is to establish a reliable end-to-end demonstration under realistic lighting and capture conditions.

- **Lighting:** Experiment with vertical camera testing to reduce the impact of strong overhead lighting. Since the actual deployment environment cannot be assumed to be dark, testing should reflect realistic ambient lighting conditions.
- **Capture range:** Determine the maximum distance at which the system can reliably capture a usable palm ROI.
- **End-to-end workflow:** Keep the demonstration concise and predictable. Plan a complete sequence covering image capture, recognition, and result verification. Include both genuine-user verification and impostor testing.
- **Autofocus:** Investigate whether enabling autofocus improves image quality and capture reliability.

### 3.2 Future Research Exploration

The team discussed several potential directions for improving deployment efficiency and recognition robustness.

- **Resolution reduction:** Investigate how far the input resolution can be reduced while maintaining acceptable recognition performance.
- **Recovery network:** Explore the feasibility of incorporating a recovery network to compensate for degraded input quality.
- **Research consultation:** Contact Prof. Bob Zhang's former student who worked on PalmIR to seek guidance on implementation and potential research extensions.
- **Lightweight deployment:** Explore model and pipeline optimizations for resource-constrained edge devices.

### 3.3 Demonstration Opportunity

The university's Open Day, expected in early January 2027, may provide an opportunity to demonstrate the project. The team should prioritize a stable, efficient, and easy-to-understand end-to-end workflow in preparation.
