# P2-CTB: cross-device palm-template transfer boundary

## Summary

Superseded on 2026-08-31: P2-CTB is not a defensible active FYP direction. A generated standardised ROI evaluated by B's matcher bypasses target capture, ROI and PAD; it therefore does not measure a device/sensor/presentation pathway. Yan 2024 materially occupies the remaining template-to-image-to-matcher endpoint. X-Palm's compound scanner-to-phone domain makes a null non-identifying, not a transfer-security barrier. The residual attribution-sufficiency audit was independently killed as routine evaluation hygiene rather than a research contribution.

## Findings

### Second-round reversal [KILL]

The second independent validation found that P2's formal rule applies `f_B` directly to a generated ROI. It is a digital matcher-input test and cannot support a physical target-device/sensor claim without capture, ROI and PAD controls. Yan's cross-algorithm/cross-dataset reconstruction-and-matching evaluation makes that remaining endpoint materially occupied. A Pi changes resource accounting only.

X-Palm is useful for recognition-domain benchmarking but its phone domain jointly varies device, pose, illumination, distance, background, occlusion and surface condition. Its standardised ROI protocol therefore does not identify a named device, sensor or protection factor. A near-FAR null may arise from decoder weakness, normalisation/ROI mismatch, compound domain shift or target-model robustness. It cannot honestly be called a finite device/security barrier.

### Direct collisions [K]

Yan et al. (Pattern Recognition 155, 2024, article 110655) present a black-box ProGAN reconstruction for handcrafted and deep palm templates, with cross-algorithm and cross-dataset evaluation. Their publisher page states that training used a 3090Ti and took approximately one day, which is a feasibility warning for an undergraduate reproduction.

Yang et al. (Knowledge-Based Systems, 2023, article 110310) study cross-database attacks against different coding-based palm templates. EMPalm (arXiv v1, 2025-10-08) studies electromagnetic leakage from seven prototype and two commercial devices. CAAP (arXiv v1, 2026-04-08) evaluates capture-aware physical patches across models and datasets, including real print-and-capture experiments. These sources make “a transferable palm attack” too broad to defend.

### No surviving residual endpoint [KILL]

The narrow attribution-sufficiency residual was evaluated separately. ISO/IEC 19795-10:2024 already requires a differential-performance plan that names variables, manipulated/fixed/blocked factors, controls and uncertainty; generic shift-diagnosis work makes data-source explanation a mature method family. X-Palm does not contain the cross-classified metadata needed to identify a named device or sensor effect. Its most honest output is an ISO-style broad-domain report plus a metadata-support manifest, which is useful preflight material but not a standalone FYP thesis.

### Data and edge feasibility [K]/[GAP]

The X-Palm repository documents 6,006 images from 103 people, 81 paired scanner/smartphone participants, 80+ smartphone models and self-contained PyTorch scripts. Its academic EULA prohibits redistribution and re-identification. This supports a no-new-biometric-collection route, but approval, data download and model compatibility remain [GAP]. A small linear/MLP decoder is the minimum CPU-feasible attack; a desktop GPU is optional for a stronger comparison. The edge board is used for target inference and resource measurement, not attack training.

## Insights

- Cross-device robustness, template privacy and attack transfer should be measured as separate axes. A good EER on B does not imply protection against a leaked A template.
- A failed transfer is only a security boundary after controlling ROI, spectrum, normalization, quantization and target threshold; otherwise it is merely a domain mismatch.
- P1 and P3 are useful audit dimensions for P2 but are too broad as independent algorithm theses. P4 black-box feedback and P5 uncertainty are controls or pivots, not the core claim.

## Strongest objection

The direct-neighbor chain may already contain the same endpoint under different names, or a weak same-device decoder may make cross-device failure uninterpretable. The protocol therefore requires same-device sanity, simple baselines, identity-disjoint splits, target FAR freezing and a kill gate before any transfer claim.

## Open questions

- Can any separately licensed palm dataset provide repeated, cross-classified device/nuisance support rather than a compound domain label? None was identified in this audit.
